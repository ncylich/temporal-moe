Why one load per layer is not much faster than Skliar's bursts

Traced on the phone, one 48-token run per arm, context 1,024, shared expert first. Every layer that loads an expert stalls, because a read takes about 4 times longer than the computation meant to hide it. Once every load stalls, the stall is roughly proportional to the number of loads, so only the average matters, and the averages are matched.

Reading it from first principles

  1. A hard cap of one load per layer pays off only if one load fits under the layer's computation and two do not. Here one does not fit: 0.21 ms of resident compute against 0.81 ms for the read.
  2. The read is slow because of latency, not volume. Each of the six reads takes about 423 us whatever its size, and waits about 152 us for a worker first.
  3. The other 1.3 ms of each layer (attention, norms, the shared expert) cannot hide the read: the router needs the attention output before it knows which expert to load. Moving the shared expert first bought about 0.08 ms of the window.
  4. So both methods stall on every layer that loads, and the stall scales with the loads: two experts cost 1.8 times one, three cost 2.3 times. Skliar's bursts are paid back by the 25% of its layers that load nothing. Net difference: +0.65 ms per token out of about 30.
  5. What is left of the difference is not the bursts. A single load stalls Skliar's run for 495 us against 421 us for the trained model, because its reads take longer (475 against 423 us each). The two runs use different model files; the cause was not looked into.
  6. The gap would open where a layer computes for longer than one read takes: a larger model, or a faster read path.
